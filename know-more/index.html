How Steady works

A kind voice, a clear map,
and 15 minutes at a time

Talking becomes a map. The map becomes practice. Practice gives you your life back. Here's each step.

From first word to lasting change

01

Talk

You talk about what loops. Steady listens and asks the next kind question. No forms. No quiz. Just a conversation.

02

Map

Your words become a simple map: what sets the loop off, what it makes you do. Then a gentle ladder of practice steps — easy ones first. You approve every step.

03

Practice

You say “start”. For 15 minutes, a warm voice helps you face the thought without feeding it. Pause or stop anytime, with one word.

04

Feel it change

Worries that peaked at 8 start topping out at 3. You watch it happen, week by week, in plain words. Proof beats hope.

The science

Warm on the outside. Rigorous underneath.

Steady uses exposure practice (ERP) — the approach therapists trust most for OCD and looping thoughts, backed by decades of research. The idea is simple: answer the loop, and your brain thinks the alarm was real. Let the alarm ring without answering, and your brain learns the truth. Steady makes the second one doable.

Why speaking beats typing

Rumination is verbal — it runs in your inner voice. Practising out loud works the exact channel the loop lives on.

Why 15 minutes

Long enough for the wave to rise, peak, and fall while you're still there to feel it fall. That falling part is where your brain rewires.

Why no quick answers

Reassurance is relief now, loop forever. The lasting calm comes from discovering you never needed the answer. Steady walks you there.

What Steady never does

The guardrails aren’t fine print. They’re the product.

It never feeds the loop

Steady won't answer “but am I sure?” — quick relief is what keeps the question coming back. It helps you stop needing the answer. That's the kindest thing in the product.

It never starts without you

Every practice needs your okay and the word “start”, said out loud. You drive. Steady navigates.

It never plays therapist

Steady is a practice companion, not a therapist — and it says so. It works great alongside a real one. See our therapists page.

It never keeps going in a crisis

Mention crisis or self-harm and Steady stops everything and points you to real humans: 988 (US), 999 (UK), your local services.
